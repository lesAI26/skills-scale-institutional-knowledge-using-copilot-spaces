# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

This documentation set describes OctoAcme's lightweight, cross-functional approach to managing projects. It is customer-first and iterative: teams validate the need, plan small shippable increments, deliver with clear ownership, make data-informed decisions, and improve through feedback and retrospectives.

## Project management process at a glance

Projects start with **initiation**: define the problem, SMART objective, success metrics, stakeholders, initial timeline, risks, and resource needs in a project one-pager. Once stakeholders agree on priority and team availability is confirmed, the work moves into **planning**. The team holds a kickoff, prioritizes and estimates backlog items with acceptance criteria, agrees on a Definition of Done, identifies dependencies, and maps milestones and releases.

During **execution and tracking**, work moves across a shared project board in short, testable increments. Standups, weekly delivery syncs, demos, pull-request review, automated checks, and status updates make progress visible and surface blockers early. The Project Manager coordinates schedules, risks, dependencies, and communications; the Product Manager owns outcomes and prioritization; developers build and test; QA validates acceptance; and stakeholders provide input and approvals. Risks are recorded with owners and mitigations, reviewed regularly, and escalated through the PM and Product Lead to the sponsor when necessary.

For **release and deployment**, OctoAcme expects acceptance criteria to be met, pull requests merged, CI and security scans passing, release notes drafted, smoke tests prepared, and rollback or mitigation plans documented. Teams validate in staging, deploy to production (preferably through an automated pipeline), verify the deployment, and announce the release. After a sprint, release, milestone, or incident, a retrospective records learnings and assigns a small number of measurable improvement actions to owners and due dates.
